Q.4 (1) • CO-03 Bloom's Level: BL2 6 Marks

1. What is knowledge representation? Write its four desirable properties.

1. Definition of Knowledge Representation (KR)

Knowledge Representation (KR) in Artificial Intelligence is the study of how an intelligent agent's beliefs, intentions, and real-world facts can be expressed formally in a symbolic structure so that a computer system can reason with that knowledge to solve complex problems, infer new facts, and make intelligent decisions.

Instead of storing raw, disconnected data, a Knowledge Representation and Reasoning (KR&R) system bridges the gap between the real world (objects, events, relationships, rules) and computational symbolic reasoning via a Knowledge Base (KB) and an Inference Engine.

Figure 4.1: Architecture of a Knowledge Representation & Reasoning (KR&R) System
2. Four Desirable Properties of a Good Knowledge Representation System

According to Rich & Knight's foundational AI framework, a good Knowledge Representation system must possess the following four desirable properties:

  1. Representational Adequacy: It is the ability to represent all kinds of knowledge that are needed in a specific domain (such as static facts, rules, default assumptions, uncertainty, and meta-knowledge).
  2. Inferential Adequacy: It is the ability to manipulate the represented structures in such a way as to derive (infer) new structures corresponding to new knowledge inferred from old knowledge.
  3. Inferential Efficiency: It is the ability to incorporate additional guidance or heuristic information into the knowledge structure that can focus the inference mechanisms in the most promising directions, avoiding exponential search delays.
  4. Acquisitional Efficiency: It is the ability to acquire new information easily—either by direct insertion from human experts or automatically through machine learning modules—without breaking existing knowledge.

Q.4 (2) • CO-03 Bloom's Level: BL2 6 Marks

2. Differentiate between procedural knowledge and declarative knowledge.

1. Conceptual Overview & Diagram

In Artificial Intelligence, knowledge stored in an intelligent system is broadly classified into Declarative Knowledge (knowing "what" is true) and Procedural Knowledge (knowing "how" to perform a task).

Figure 4.2: Classification and Characteristics of Declarative vs. Procedural Knowledge
2. Point-by-Point Comparison Table (6 Marks)
Parameter Declarative Knowledge Procedural Knowledge
1. Core Meaning Represents "What is true" — passive facts, assertions, concepts, and relationships about the world. Represents "How to do something" — step-by-step instructions, operational procedures, and control strategies.
2. Mode of Representation Expressed using declarative statements in Propositional Logic, First-Order Logic (FOL), Semantic Networks, or Frames. Expressed using IF-THEN production rules, subroutines, finite-state procedures, or imperative code.
3. Ease of Modification High (Modular): Individual facts can be added, updated, or deleted independently without breaking other facts. Low (Tightly Coupled): Changing a step inside an algorithm or procedure can affect the entire execution flow.
4. Execution Efficiency Slower: Requires a general-purpose inference engine to search and combine facts at runtime. Faster: Directly executes pre-compiled steps or domain-specific heuristics without heavy search overhead.
5. Explanation & Debugging Easy to inspect, verify, and explain explicitly to human users. Harder to inspect or explain because control flow is embedded inside code/rules.
6. Classic Examples "Snow is white", "Dog ISA Mammal", or a database table of cities and distances. A sorting algorithm (e.g., Merge Sort), how to solve a Rubik's cube, or how to ride a bicycle.
💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho (Easy Difference)

Declarative vs Procedural Knowledge ka farq ek line mein samjho:

  • Declarative Knowledge ("KYA HAI / WHAT"): Yeh sirf facts aur information batati hai. Jaise "Snow is white" ya "2 + 2 = 4". Ismein yeh nahi likha hota ki kaam kaise karna hai, bas sachai (statement) store hoti hai. Isko edit ya update karna bahut easy hota hai.
  • Procedural Knowledge ("KAISE KARNA HAI / HOW"): Yeh किसी task ko perform karne ka step-by-step tarika (algorithm/process) batati hai. Jaise "Array ko sort kaise karein (Sorting Algorithm)" ya "Chai banane ki recipe". Yeh execute hone mein fast hoti hai kyunki steps fix hote hain, par beech mein modify karna thoda mushkil hota hai.
Q.4 (3) • CO-03 Bloom's Level: BL2 6 Marks

3. Represent the following statements in First-Order Logic: (a) All humans are mortal. (b) Some cats can fly.

1. Introduction to First-Order Logic (FOL) Translation

First-Order Logic (FOL) (also known as Predicate Logic) extends Propositional Logic by introducing objects, variables (x, y), predicates (properties/relations), and quantifiers:

  • Universal Quantifier (∀x): Means "For all x" or "Every x". Almost always paired with the implication connective (→).
  • Existential Quantifier (∃x): Means "There exists at least one x" or "For some x". Almost always paired with the conjunction connective (∧).
2. Statement (a): "All humans are mortal." (3 Marks)

Step 1 — Identify Predicates:

  • Let Human(x) be the predicate meaning: "x is a human."
  • Let Mortal(x) be the predicate meaning: "x is mortal."

Step 2 — Identify Quantifier & Logical Connective:

  • The keyword "All" requires the Universal Quantifier (∀x).
  • In English, "All humans are mortal" means: "For every object x in the universe of discourse, IF x is a human, THEN x is mortal."

Step 3 — Final First-Order Logic Representation:

∀x [ Human(x) → Mortal(x) ]
// Read as: "For all x, if x is a Human, then x is Mortal."
3. Statement (b): "Some cats can fly." (3 Marks)

Step 1 — Identify Predicates:

  • Let Cat(x) be the predicate meaning: "x is a cat."
  • Let CanFly(x) (or Fly(x)) be the predicate meaning: "x can fly."

Step 2 — Identify Quantifier & Logical Connective:

  • The keyword "Some" requires the Existential Quantifier (∃x).
  • In English, "Some cats can fly" means: "There exists at least one object x such that x is a cat AND x can fly."

Step 3 — Final First-Order Logic Representation:

∃x [ Cat(x) ∧ CanFly(x) ]
// Read as: "There exists some x such that x is a Cat AND x can Fly."
Figure 4.3: Set-Theoretic / Venn Diagram Interpretation of Universal (∀) vs. Existential (∃) FOL Statements
💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho (FOL Golden Rule)

Exam mein FOL likhne ka sabse important Golden Rule yaad rakho:

  • Rule 1 — "All / Every" (∀ ke saath hamesha → lagta hai):
    Jab sentence mein "All humans are mortal" bola hai, toh hum bol rahe hain: Duniya mein koi bhi x utha lo (∀x), AGAR woh Human(x) hai, TOH (→) woh pakka Mortal(x) hoga. Isliye answer hai: ∀x (Human(x) → Mortal(x)).
  • Rule 2 — "Some / At least one" (∃ ke saath hamesha ∧ [AND] lagta hai):
    Jab sentence mein "Some cats can fly" bola hai, toh iska matlab hai: Kam se kam ek aisa x exist karta hai (∃x) jo Cat(x) BHI HAI AUR (∧) CanFly(x) BHI HAI. Isliye answer hai: ∃x (Cat(x) ∧ CanFly(x)).
Q.4 (4) • CO-03 Bloom's Level: BL2 6 Marks

4. Differentiate between forward chaining and backward chaining.

1. Overview of Rule-Based Inference

In an AI Inference Engine using Horn Clauses / IF-THEN production rules, there are two primary reasoning strategies to prove a conclusion from a Knowledge Base: Forward Chaining (Data-Driven Reasoning) and Backward Chaining (Goal-Driven Reasoning).

Figure 4.4: Directional Flow of Forward Chaining (Facts → Goal) vs. Backward Chaining (Goal → Facts)
2. Detailed Comparison Table (6 Marks)
Parameter Forward Chaining Backward Chaining
1. Starting Point Starts from the known initial facts in the Knowledge Base and works forward toward a conclusion/goal. Starts from the target goal (hypothesis) and works backward to check if supporting facts exist in the KB.
2. Reasoning Approach Data-Driven (Bottom-Up reasoning). Goal-Driven (Top-Down reasoning).
3. Rule Matching Matches facts against the left-hand side (IF / antecedent) of rules and adds the right-hand side to the KB. Matches the goal against the right-hand side (THEN / consequent) of rules and sets the IF-conditions as new sub-goals.
4. Search Strategy Typically uses Breadth-First Search (BFS) exploration across all applicable rules. Typically uses Depth-First Search (DFS) to resolve sub-goals recursively.
5. Number of Inferences May generate many irrelevant facts/conclusions that are not related to a specific query. Highly focused; only evaluates rules that are directly relevant to proving the requested goal.
6. Typical Applications Planning, process monitoring, control systems, and production expert systems (e.g., CLIPS, OPS5). Medical diagnosis (e.g., MYCIN), troubleshooting, theorem proving, and Prolog queries.
💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho (Forward vs Backward Chaining)

Real-life detective ya doctor ke example se samjho:

  • Forward Chaining (Facts → Goal | Data-Driven):
    Yahan hum Known Facts se shuru karte hain. Maan lo aapko pata hai: "Patient ko fever hai (Fact A)" aur "Body ache hai (Fact B)". Ab aap rules apply karke aage badhte ho ki in facts se kya-kya naye conclusions nikal sakte hain jab tak final disease (Goal) na mil jaye. Isko Bottom-Up / Data-Driven kehte hain.
  • Backward Chaining (Goal → Facts | Goal-Driven):
    Yahan hum seedha Goal (Hypothesis) se ulta shuru karte hain. Maan lo doctor ko shak hai: "Kya patient ko Malaria hai? (Goal Z)". Ab doctor peeche check karega ki Malaria hone ke liye kaunse symptoms (sub-goals) chahiye, aur phir verify karega ki kya woh symptoms patient (Known Facts) mein maujood hain ya nahi. Isko Top-Down / Goal-Driven kehte hain.
Q.4 (5) • CO-03 Bloom's Level: BL2 6 Marks

5. Write the steps of the resolution method (proof by refutation).

1. Principle of Resolution (Proof by Refutation / Contradiction)

Resolution (introduced by J. Alan Robinson in 1965) is a complete inference procedure used in Propositional Logic and First-Order Logic. It works on the principle of Proof by Refutation (Proof by Contradiction): To prove that a goal statement G follows from a Knowledge Base (KB), we assume the negation of the goal (¬G) is true, add it to the KB, and repeatedly resolve clauses with complementary literals until we derive the Empty Clause (□ or NIL / False), which proves a contradiction and confirms that G is true.

Figure 4.5: Resolution Proof by Refutation Flowchart & Resolution Tree
2. Step-by-Step Procedure of the Resolution Method (6 Marks)
  1. Step 1 — Convert Knowledge Base (KB) to Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF):
    Transform all given statements in the Knowledge Base into clause form (CNF)—a conjunction of clauses where each clause is a disjunction (OR, ∨) of literals:
    • Eliminate implications (replace A → B with ¬A ∨ B) and biconditionals.
    • Move negation (¬) inward using De Morgan's laws and quantifier negation rules.
    • Standardize variables apart, eliminate existential quantifiers (∃) via Skolemization, drop universal quantifiers (∀), and distribute ∨ over ∧.
  2. Step 2 — Add the Negation of the Goal (¬G) to the KB:
    Negate the statement/goal G that needs to be proved (forming ¬G), convert ¬G into CNF clause form, and add it to the set of clauses in the Knowledge Base.
  3. Step 3 — Select Two Clauses and Apply the Resolution Rule (with Unification):
    Pick two clauses that contain complementary literals (e.g., L in one clause and ¬L in another clause; in FOL, unify predicates using Most General Unifier θ). Cancel out the complementary pair and combine the remaining literals to form a new clause called the Resolvent:
    Clause 1: (A ∨ B)   ,   Clause 2: (¬B ∨ C)
    ──────────────────────────────────────────
    Resolvent: (A ∨ C)   // B and ¬B cancel each other out
  4. Step 4 — Add Resolvent & Repeat Until Stopping Condition:
    Add the newly generated resolvent clause to the KB and repeat Step 3 until one of two conditions occurs:
    • Empty Clause (□ / NIL) is derived: A contradiction is reached (e.g., resolving Q and ¬Q). Since adding ¬G caused a contradiction, the original goal G is proved to be TRUE.
    • No new clauses can be derived: If the empty clause cannot be produced and no more pairs can be resolved, then the goal G cannot be proved from the given KB.
💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho (Resolution Method — Proof by Refutation)

Resolution Method kaise kaam karta hai?
Isko "Ulta Maan Kar Galat Sabit Karna" (Proof by Contradiction/Refutation) kehte hain. Maan lo humein prove karna hai ki Q sach hai:

  • Step 1 (CNF Conversion): Sabse pehle Knowledge Base ke saare sentences ko simple OR (∨) waale clauses mein convert kar lo (jaise P → Q ban jata hai ¬P ∨ Q).
  • Step 2 (Negate the Goal): Jis Goal ko prove karna hai, uska ulta (Negation, jaise ¬Q) maan lo aur usko Knowledge Base mein add kar do.
  • Step 3 (Cancel Opposite Literals): Ab do-do clauses uthao jinmein opposite terms hon (jaise ek mein P aur doosre mein ¬P) aur unhe cancel karke naya clause (Resolvent) banao.
  • Step 4 (Empty Clause □ = Proof Complete!): Cancel karte-karte jab aakhir mein Q aur ¬Q aapas mein kat jayein aur Empty Clause (□ / kuch nahi bache) aa jaye, toh iska matlab humara ulta maanna (¬Q) galat tha — yaani Goal Q 100% TRUE hai!
